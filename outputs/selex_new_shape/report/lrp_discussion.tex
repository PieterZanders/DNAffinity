\paragraph{Feature importance: which MD parameters, and what do they mean?}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Which MD parameters.} In the MD-only models, 57--64\% of the relevance sits on \texttt{fce} off-diagonal couplings, 18\% on \texttt{fce} diagonal (stiffness of single parameters) and 18--25\% on \texttt{avg} (mean geometry). Within \texttt{avg}, TILT is the most used parameter in both models (3.9\% DFN (e), 5.8\% CNN1D (e)); the largest couplings are TILT--ROLL (10.6\% DFN), SLIDE--TILT (6.1\% CNN1D) and ROLL--TWIST (5.3\% CNN1D). RISE has the clearest positive signed effect on strong binders (+0.216 DFN (e), +0.120 CNN1D (e)). The stiffness (covariance) terms are thus used more than the mean geometry, consistent with the data containing a sequence-dependent deformability signal. Roll/twist/slide deformability is a plausible indirect-readout signature for an ETS-type GGAA site, which is recognised with DNA bending.
\item \textbf{Why this is not proof of physics.} Because every shape value is a deterministic function of the tetramer, LRP credit on a parameter can reflect that this parameter helps to tell tetramers apart, not that the TF senses it. The permuted-table control (Section~\ref{sec:mdaim}) is the more direct test. It shows that the real values help beyond identity for DFN ($+0.135$) and RF ($+0.052$), and only slightly for CNN1D ($+0.022$), so the LRP ranking of parameters is most credible for the DFN. The six \texttt{avg} parameters and the 21 \texttt{fce} terms per window are also correlated with each other, and LRP shares credit between correlated channels.
\item \textbf{No GATA signal.} LRP does not show relevance concentrated on a GATA core, for a simple reason: \texttt{4.txt} contains essentially no GATA signal at count $\geq$10. Its strongest $k$-mers are ETS-class sites (ACTTCCGG/CCGGAAGT, GGAA core), and the models' relevance logos highlight exactly these bases, also for the MD-only models (core share 0.495 and 0.542 vs.\ a positional expectation of 0.417 and 0.465). Either the HT-SELEX library selected an ETS-like binder or the file is mislabelled or contaminated. This should be verified against the original SELEX run before any Gata4-specific interpretation.
\item \textbf{Shape vs.\ sequence with one-hot.} CNN1D uses one-hot most, yet it still gives about half of its relevance to shape channels in set (a). Because the shape values are a deterministic function of the 16 one-hot channels of the same window, the network can spread the same information over both, and LRP shares the credit between them. This is consistent with the identical accuracy of sets (a) and (d). Without one-hot (e), the shape block takes over the whole signal, and its relevance concentrates on the \texttt{fce} couplings.
\item \textbf{Caveats.} LRP maps for the CNN depend on the rule choice: $\gamma$ maps correlate only 0.14--0.17 with gradient$\times$input, and only about 50\% of the output is conserved. Feature-type shares are partly driven by the number of channels per group. The positional profiles mainly reflect architecture (padding for CNN1D, window coverage for the DFN), and the base-level maps of (e) are projections from window relevance.
\end{itemize}
